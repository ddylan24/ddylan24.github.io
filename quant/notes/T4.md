# T4: Election contracts vs financial markets. Hedging demand and risk premium

**Question.** If an election outcome is correlated with the stock market, hedgers will pay up for the contract that pays in bad states. Prices would then embed a risk premium and differ from probabilities. How big is it for the 2024 US presidential market?

## Data (`code/t4_election_beta.py`)
- Polymarket "Will Donald Trump win the 2024 US Presidential Election?" daily prices, Jan 15–Nov 5 2024 (198 trading days, daily sd 1.5¢).
- Yahoo daily returns for SPY, IWM, XLE, XLF, TAN, ICLN, TLT, GLD, IBIT and USD/MXN.
- **Alignment matters.** The Polymarket daily point is stamped 00:00 UTC, which is about 8pm ET of the *previous* date. The change that covers trading day D is p(label D) − p(label D−1).
  - Misaligning by one day turned the SPY correlation from +0.016 into a spurious +0.102 and flipped the signs on solar and the peso.
  - New pitfall: check timestamp conventions on *daily* data too, not just minute data (R1, R4).

## Results (betas in ¢ of contract per 1% asset move, HAC t-stats)
| asset | beta | t | corr |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | +0.03 | 0.3 | +0.02 |
| TAN (solar) | −0.07 | −1.6 | −0.11 |
| ICLN (clean energy) | −0.10 | −1.5 | −0.10 |
| USD/MXN | +0.14 | 1.5 | +0.08 |
| IBIT (bitcoin) | +0.04 | 1.1 | +0.09 |
| TLT | −0.13 | −0.8 | −0.07 |

Joint regression (R² 0.037): TAN −0.08 (t −1.9), USD/MXN +0.19 (t +1.8), IBIT +0.05 (t +1.3), SPY +0.09 (t 0.6).

- The signs match the well-known **"Trump trades"**: Trump's odds up went with solar and clean energy down, the dollar up vs the peso, and bitcoin up. But they're individually only marginally significant on daily data.
- **Market beta is about 0.** The price moves 0.03¢ per 1% SPY move (0.03$ per 100%), so the CAPM-style premium is about 100·0.031·5%·0.8y ≈ **0.1¢**. (An earlier version said 0.00¢ because of a scaling slip, corrected in U1.) Election prediction markets carry no meaningful equity risk premium. Their prices can be read as probabilities, up to the biases studied in P2/R3/S3.
- **Hedging use.** The contract is a poor hedge for broad equities (R² ≈ 0.04 even jointly). It works as a hedge only for specific policy-exposed sectors, and even there, sector moves explain little of the contract's daily variance.

## Lessons
1. Risk premia in prediction markets are negligible unless the outcome is tied to aggregate wealth (e.g., recession contracts, which deserve their own test).
2. Daily timestamps need the same care as intraday ones: an off-by-one day manufactures correlations.
