# N3 — Market making on binary contracts (simulation) + Polymarket liquidity rewards
Sim: 2-week binary, hourly; true probability = logit random walk (σ=.08/h); MM's estimate lags truth by 6h; ~2 orders/hour (Poisson); a share of orders informed (trade only when truth is outside the MM's quotes); resolves to 0/1. Uninformed demand assumed insensitive to spread (so wider always wins here — in reality competition sets the spread).
P&L per market (mean ± sd over paths) and per contract traded:
- 0% informed: half-spread .5¢ → +3.3 (+.49¢/contract); 1¢ +7.1 (+1.06¢); 2¢ +13.5; 4¢ +26.8.
- 10% informed: .5¢ +0.9 (+.13¢); 1¢ +4.5 (+.66¢); 2¢ +11.1; 4¢ +23.8.
- 30% informed: .5¢ −2.2 (−.33¢); 1¢ +1.5 (+.22¢); 2¢ +6.6; 4¢ +17.6.
→ Adverse selection eats ≈ informed share × typical mispricing per fill; break-even half-spread rises with informed share (Glosten–Milgrom, Module 11). P&L sd ≈ ±12 regardless — dominated by inventory × final 0/1 jump.
Stop quoting 0/24/72h before resolution (10% informed, 1¢): P&L +4.5 ± 12.2 / +4.6 ± 11.6 / +3.8 ± 10.3 → withdrawing before resolution trades a little profit for lower variance.
Polymarket liquidity rewards (docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards): resting limit orders within a market's max spread v and above min size score S(v,s) = ((v−s)/v)²·b per order (s = distance from size-adjusted midpoint, in cents); two-sided quotes boosted; sampled each minute; paid daily by share of total score. Quadratic → strong incentive to quote tight; rewards can make a slightly adverse-selected book profitable ("reward farming").
