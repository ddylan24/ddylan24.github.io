# W1: How stable are Kalshi LIP books? (the richest programs lasting ≥1 day)

**Method (`code/w1_lip_sample.py`, `code/w1_lip_dynamics.py`).**
- The 60 richest live liquidity programs lasting at least a day (by $/day).
- Full order books every 3 minutes for about 35–45 minutes (2026-10-15, 00:24–01:10 UTC).
- Each snapshot is scored with the official rules (`pm_toolkit.lip_side_share`).

## Results (12 snapshots × 60 markets at analysis time)
- **Excluded snapshots ≈ 0%.** 59/60 markets met Target on *both* sides in every snapshot, so the payout haircut for these pools is essentially nil. The "scheduled pool ≈ paid pool" assumption holds for rich, long programs. It's likely worse for thin or short ones.
- **The books barely move.** The best YES bid changed in 0 of 11 intervals (median), the median spread is **1¢**, and total depth varies only 3% (coefficient of variation 0.03). These are passive, parked LP books, not actively re-quoted ones.
- **The newcomer share is steady:** a Target-size order at the best bid on both sides would hold a median **21%** of the score, with only ±1.1% drift over the window.

## Interpretation
- Rich, long programs look like a **stable, crowded, low-churn equilibrium**: incumbents park depth at a 1¢ spread and leave it. A newcomer's share is predictable (~20%), so reward income is too. The open question is the fill cost of sitting at the touch, which needs fill data (home-PC paper trial, PLAYBOOK §2.5).
- Money in short programs (60% of the $290k/day; notes/LIP.md) probably behaves differently: books have to be rebuilt every hour or 15 minutes and informed flow is heavier near resolution. A separate W1-style sample at higher frequency is worth doing (added as X1).
