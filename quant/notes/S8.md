# S8: Does a public weather-model forecast beat Kalshi's temperature markets?

**Question.** S6 says beating a market takes *resolution* (information). The obvious public information source for daily-high markets is the NWS's own model output. Does it add anything beyond Kalshi prices?

## Data (`code/s8_weather_model.py`)
- **Markets:** 199 complete Kalshi city-days (NY, Chicago, Miami, LA, Austin, Denver; Aug 7–Oct 13, 2026). Mid price at local midnight starting the day (N2), de-vigged additively (best method in R5).
- **Forecast:** official **NWS GFS MOS daytime max (n_x)** from the 12Z run the day before, issued ~16Z, about 8 hours before the market snapshot, so no leakage.
  - Source: Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive `mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/mos.py?station=KNYC&model=GFS&sts=…&ets=…&format=csv`.
  - Stations match Kalshi's settlement sources: KNYC (Central Park), KMDW, KMIA, KLAX, KAUS, KDEN.
  - IEM rate-limits per IP, so cache files and retry slowly. Open-Meteo hit its *daily* limit on this shared cloud IP.
- **Model:** high ~ N(MOS + μ, σ²) with integer rounding. Bucket probability Φ((hi+½−m)/σ) − Φ((lo−½−m)/σ). μ and σ fitted per city by maximum likelihood on the first half (83 days), tested on the second half (116 days).

## Results
- Fitted MOS bias and error: AUS +2.1/1.3°F, CHI −2.0/2.2, DEN −1.4/2.4, LAX +1.9/4.3, MIA −0.4/1.2, NY +1.2/1.7. The biases are station-specific and sizable.
- Test set:

| forecast | log loss | Brier/day | Δ vs market (t) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **market** | **1.196** | **0.620** | — |
| GFS MOS model | 1.811 | 0.768 | +0.615 (4.5) |
| 75% market + 25% model | 1.229 | 0.626 | +0.033 (1.5) |
| 50/50 | 1.303 | 0.653 | +0.106 (2.8) |

- **Trading** on model–quote gaps (>3/5/10¢ plus fee) loses 1.0–2.3¢ per contract (t −0.4 to −1.3).
- **The market contains everything in yesterday's MOS and more.** By midnight, traders have the 18Z/00Z runs, NBM, HRRR and current observations. The model's fitted σ of 1.2–1.3°F in Miami and Austin was also overconfident out of sample.
- A fair test needs forecasts issued at the *same time* as the price: the 00Z MOS, NBM, or ensemble spreads. Good work for the user's home machine with live data capture (CONTEXT §5b).

## Lessons
1. Public, stale information is already in the price. An information edge must be *newer* (faster runs, better downscaling, live observations) or *better processed* (ensemble calibration, station-specific bias correction).
2. Match information timestamps exactly. Comparing an 8-hour-old forecast with a fresh price stacks the deck for the market. Comparing the other way round is look-ahead.
3. Data sources: IEM is an excellent, verified archive of NWS products (MOS, METAR, CLI reports).
