# R4: Order-flow imbalance on Polymarket. Price impact and (no) predictability

**Question.** Does signed taker flow move Polymarket prices (Kyle λ)? Does it *predict* the next minute (Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov 2014 found order-flow imbalance explains contemporaneous equity price changes, not future ones)?

## Data
- The 8 most active two-sided markets on 2026-10-14: October FOMC buckets, MLB and NFL games, the Brazil election (Lula, Flávio Bolsonaro), the US–Iran ceasefire and Netanyahu.
- Up to 3,000 taker trades each from `data-api.polymarket.com/trades`, covering about 1–2 weeks.
  - **The default is takerOnly=true**: one row per taker order, so `side` is the taker's side and signed flow is valid. I verified this: 500 rows had 500 unique tx hashes. `takerOnly=false` adds maker rows.
  - Signed YES-equivalent flow: buy YES or sell NO = +, otherwise −, in $.
- 1-minute bars. Script: `code/r4_ofi.py [raw|shift|trade]`.

## A data trap: `prices-history` lags about a minute
- The fidelity=1 points are stamped at :16–:20 seconds past the minute and reflect the state about a minute earlier.
- corr(flow_t, Δp_{t+k}) for k = −2…3:
  - prices-history: 0.07, −0.01, 0.20, **0.63** (k = +1), 0.12, 0.07
  - trade prices: −0.01, 0.13, **0.42** (k = 0), 0.08, 0.04, 0.07
- Taken at face value, raw history says flow "predicts" the next minute in **8/8 markets (median t 4.5, up to 40)**. That's an artifact. Shifting history back one minute removes it (only 12% of markets have |t| > 2).
- **Always cross-check a vendor's timestamps against trades before believing a lead-lag result.**

## Results (aligned)
| | price impact λ (¢ per $1k), same minute | t | next-minute coefficient | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fed Oct +25bp | 0.11 | 4.3 | +0.024 (trade px) / +0.005 (shifted) | 2.4 / 0.7 |
| Fed Oct no change | 0.03 | 3.8 | +0.010 / 0.000 | 2.4 / −0.1 |
| Netanyahu next PM | 0.06–0.08 | 5.5–6.5 | ≈0 | — |
| Seahawks–Broncos | 0.005 | 19–46 | ≈0 | — |
| **median (8 markets)** | **0.024–0.030** | 4.1 | +0.002–0.004 | 1.1–2.0 |

- **Impact is real and differs ~20× across markets.** $10k of net buying moves the Fed +25bp contract about 1.1¢ but an NFL game only 0.05¢. Sports books are much deeper per dollar. This matches the book-depth results in M3.
- **Predictability is negligible.** The leftover continuation is at most ~20% of impact (Fed +25bp: 0.024 vs 0.111) and significant only with trade prices. That's consistent with order splitting or slow incorporation, but $10k of flow implies ≤0.24¢ of drift, far below the 1¢ spread. **Not tradable.**
- Polymarket behaves like Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov's equities: flow explains same-period moves, not future ones.

## Lessons
1. Kyle λ ≈ 0.03¢ per $1k median here. Use it for sizing: the impact of a $Q order ≈ λ·Q, on top of half the spread.
2. Check timestamp alignment before any lead-lag study. A one-minute lag in a price series manufactures "alpha" with t = 40.
3. The data-api default is takerOnly=true, so `side` is the taker's side.
