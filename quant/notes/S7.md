# S7: Polymarket FOMC odds vs fed funds futures

**Question.** Does Polymarket's "Fed decision in October 2026" agree with the professional market's pricing in CBOT 30-day fed funds futures?

## Math
- ZQ futures settle at 100 − (average daily EFFR over the contract month).
- The FOMC meets Oct 27–28 and the new rate takes effect Oct 29, so 3 of October's 31 days are post-meeting and all of November is.
  - From the November contract: E[Δ] = (100 − ZQX) − EFFR_now.
  - From the October contract: E[Δ] = [(100 − ZQV) − EFFR_now] · 31/3.
- If the only outcomes are hold or +25bp, P(hike) = E[Δ]/25bp.
- Assumes EFFR stays at the same spot inside the target range (it's 3.88% in 3.75–4.00) and no term premium. Both assumptions are standard for CME-FedWatch-style calculations.

## Data (`code/s7_fomc_futures.py`, 2026-10-14)
- FRED EFFR 3.88% (10/13). Yahoo ZQV26 96.1125, ZQX26 96.0450, ZQZ26 95.9000.
- Polymarket mids: no change 0.705, +25bp 0.285, +50bp 0.0055, −25bp 0.0035, −50bp 0.0015 (Σ 1.000).
- FRED needs curl; urllib got "Remote end closed connection".

## Result
| source | E[Δ] at Oct meeting | P(+25bp) |
|---|---|---|
| Fed funds futures, Nov contract | +7.5bp | 0.30 |
| Fed funds futures, Oct contract | +7.7bp | 0.31 |
| Polymarket (all buckets) | **+7.2bp** | 0.285 |

- **The two agree within 0.3bp**, about 1¢ of probability, below Polymarket's 1¢ spread.
- With Q2 (6-second reaction to the September decision) and Q3 (BTC threshold markets matching the options surface), that makes three independent checks: **liquid Polymarket macro and crypto markets are tied to the professional markets within about a spread.**
- The Dec contract (95.90, so a 4.10% December average) implies a further ~+15bp expected by the December 9 meeting. That's a forecastable next check.

## Lessons
1. Read Fed probabilities straight off ZQ futures with the two formulas above. You don't need CME FedWatch (which was blocked here).
2. Any "edge" in liquid FOMC prediction markets must beat a professional market already within 1¢. Look instead at less-liquid derivative questions (dissent counts, statement wording), where no futures market exists.
