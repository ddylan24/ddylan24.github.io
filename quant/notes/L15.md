# L1 — Merton jump-diffusion
Daily log return density: Σ_k Pois(k; λΔ)·N(μΔ + kμ_J, σ²Δ + kσ_J²). MLE on CRSP daily 2000–2026: σ 10.0%/yr, λ 110/yr, μ_J −.21%, σ_J 1.53%; jumps = 72% of variance. LogL normal 19,962 vs JD 20,809 (Δ 847). Interpretation: MLE uses frequent small "jumps" to mimic volatility clustering, not rare crashes.
Option pricing: Merton series Σ_n e^{−λ'T}(λ'T)^n/n!·BS(σ_n, r_n), λ' = λ(1+k), k = e^{μ_J+σ_J²/2} − 1, σ_n² = σ² + nσ_J²/T, r_n = r − λk + n·ln(1+k)/T.
Physical params → nearly flat smile (20.5% → 18.7%), RMSE 6.35% vs market. Calibrated to the 30d SPY smile (risk-neutral): σ 9.4%, λ 1.12/yr, mean jump −10.7%, σ_J 7.6% → 30.1 26.1 19.9 16.2 13.3 11.8 11.1 vs market 30.8 25.1 19.3 16.7 14.5 11.4 10.4: RMSE .77% (Heston 1.31%).
→ Options price about one −11% crash per year — far more than history: crash risk premium (D3). Jumps explain short-dated skew better than stochastic vol (Bates combines both).
# L5 — Value's long drawdown (HML from KF daily, monthly compounded, 1926-07 → 2026-08)
Sharpe .35. 2006-12 peak → 2020-09 trough: −56%; NOT back to the 2006 peak as of 2026-08. Previous worst before 2007: −44% (1935-03).
Block bootstrap of pre-2007 HML (geometric blocks mean 24m, 2000 sims of 100 yrs): P(max DD ≥ 56%) = 13.2% → extreme but not inconsistent with its own past.
Pre-2007 Sharpe .46 ⇒ P(loss over 14 yrs) = 4.3% (iid normal). Lesson: even century-old premia can lose for 14+ years; drawdown expectations should come from block bootstraps, not normal formulas. (Debate: value spread widening, intangibles — Arnott et al. 2021, Lev & Srivastava.)
