# Q2: Speed of information. Polymarket around the 16 Sep 2026 FOMC

**Question.** How fast does a prediction market absorb a scheduled public announcement? Is there leakage beforehand? How does it compare with Treasuries and equities?

## Event
- FOMC statement at **2026-09-16 18:00:00 UTC** (2:00pm ET). The Fed **hiked 25bp**, verified by FRED `DFEDTARU`: 3.75 → 4.00 effective 9/17.
- Polymarket event "Fed Decision in September?" (slug `fed-decision-in-september-762`, neg-risk, 5 buckets, **$208M volume**).

## Data
- Tick trades from `data-api.polymarket.com/trades?market=<conditionId>&limit=500&offset=k`. Results come newest first; I paged until 3h before the event. Timestamps are on-chain block times (Polygon ~2s blocks; matching happens off-chain slightly earlier).
- Hourly and daily `prices-history`. The hourly range must be ≤ ~7 days per call; a 30-day range returns 400.
- Yahoo 1-minute bars for SPY, TLT, SHY, ^IRX and ^TNX. **These are only available for the last 30 days.** I got them on day 28.
- Script: `code/q2_fomc_speed.py`.

## Results
**Ahead of the meeting**
- The hike probability was 0.28 in mid-May. It rose to 0.54 on Sep 3 and 0.885 on the day.
- The largest hourly jumps were at 13:00 UTC (8:30–9am ET) on Sep 4, 10 and 11. Those are data-release mornings (jobs report, CPI), so the market moved on macro data, not leaks.
- Last hour before the statement: $6.7M traded at a VWAP of 0.861. Last minute: 0.878.
- No sign of leakage, since the price was flat at 0.86–0.88 into the release.

**The announcement (25bp-hike YES price)**
| t (s) | −60 | −10 | 0 | +5 | +10 | +60 | +120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hike | 0.880 | 0.870 | 0.870 | 0.899 | 0.990 | 0.990 | 0.999 |
| no change | 0.130 | 0.130 | 0.140 | 0.010 | 0.010 | 0.010 | 0.001 |

- **90% of the move was done in 6 seconds** (≈ 2–3 Polygon blocks). The last cent took about 2 minutes.
- Fills at stale prices (YES < 0.99) in the first 5 minutes: 22 trades, $270k, at 0.88–0.99.
- Taker P&L vs resolution across all buckets in the first 5 minutes was only about **$5.6k** on $3.8M of notional, so very little was picked off. Caveat: the data-api lists fills per participant, so which side was the taker is ambiguous and rows repeat.
- **Implied surprise:** (1 − 0.87) × 25bp ≈ 3bp of unexpected tightening, which is tiny.

**Cross-asset reaction (1-minute bars)**
- First minute: TLT +0.17%, ^IRX −1.6bp, SPY −0.08%. Bonds briefly *rallied* on a hike that was 87% expected (sell the rumor, buy the fact, or dovish wording).
- Over 60 minutes, through the 2:30 press conference: ^TNX +5.9bp, ^IRX +3.2bp, SHY −0.21%, SPY **−0.84%**.
- SHY 1-minute return sd went from 0.45bp before to 1.20bp in the 30 minutes after (×2.7).
- **The Polymarket decision contract only measures the decision, so it's done in seconds.** The macro information (guidance, dots, press conference) took about an hour to price, and that's where markets moved most.

## Lessons
1. Scheduled public news reaches a liquid prediction market within seconds. Trading it needs sub-second infrastructure and co-located feeds. A retail reaction is far too slow.
2. A well-anticipated outcome leaves almost nothing for stale-quote pickoffs. Here it was ~$6k on $208M of volume.
3. Prediction markets measure *one* variable. To trade the full announcement, you need the instruments that price guidance too (Treasury futures, SOFR options).
4. Before the meeting, the big moves came from data releases. A model of how CPI/NFP surprises map into Fed-decision probabilities is the more realistic edge than reacting to the decision itself.
5. Yahoo minute data disappears after 30 days. Save it the same week.
