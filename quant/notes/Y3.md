# Y3: Pricing Kalshi 15-minute FX binaries from spot (a failed model, and why)

**Idea.** Fair P(YES) at each fill = N(ln(S_t/K) / (σ√τ)), using Yahoo 1-minute spot for S_t, the official target K, τ in minutes and rolling 1-minute vol. Compare fills to fair value. Script: `code/y3_fx15_fair.py`; ≈110k/100k fills for EUR/USD and USD/JPY.

## Result
- The model is **worse than the market** on outcomes. Brier: EUR/USD model **0.314** vs fill prices **0.148**; USD/JPY 0.166 vs 0.151.
- The "taker pays 3.1–3.5¢ over fair" it produces is therefore an artifact. Realized taker P&L to settlement is about 0 (Y1).

## Why it fails
- A 15-minute digital's delta near expiry is huge (S9: vol ∝ φ(N⁻¹p)/√τ; Q3: about 4¢ per $100 for BTC at 2 days). For FX over the last few minutes, **one pip of spot is worth several cents of probability**.
- Kalshi settles on its own benchmark source. Yahoo's minute bars are a different, coarser feed (timestamps to the minute, bid/mid differences, a different aggregator). That basis is comparable to the whole move.
- To price these you'd need the **exact settlement feed at sub-second resolution**, which a cloud session doesn't have. That's another job for the home PC.

## Lesson
Before pricing a short-dated binary from an underlying, use the *settlement source* at sub-second resolution. Check the model's calibration against outcomes *before* trusting its "edge". Here the check caught it immediately.
