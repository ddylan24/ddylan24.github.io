# S5: Kelly betting across mutually exclusive outcomes

**Setting.** n outcomes, exactly one wins (temperature buckets, election candidates). YES on i costs p_i and pays $1. Your beliefs are q_i. Spend fraction f_i of wealth on i and keep cash c = 1 − Σf_i. If i wins, wealth = c + f_i/p_i. Maximize G = Σ q_i ln(c + f_i/p_i).

## Closed form (Kelly 1956, horse-race case)
1. Sort outcomes by edge ratio q_i/p_i, descending.
2. Add outcomes to the bet set S while q_i/p_i > R_S, where **R_S = (1 − Σ_S q) / (1 − Σ_S p)**.
3. Stakes: **f_i = q_i − R·p_i** for i ∈ S, and cash kept = R.

Derivation sketch: the first-order conditions give c + f_i/p_i = q_i/(λ p_i) on S and c = (1 − Σ_S q)/λ off S. With λ = 1 (since Σf + c = 1), c = R.

## Results (`code/s5_kelly_multi.py`)
- Example: prices (0.03, 0.12, 0.35, 0.33, 0.15, 0.06), Σ 1.04, and beliefs (0.02, 0.10, 0.30, 0.40, 0.15, 0.03).
  - Closed form: stake **11.44% on the 0.33 bucket and 2.02% on the 0.15 bucket**, keep 86.5% cash. Growth 0.01189 per round, identical to numeric SLSQP.
  - **Surprise:** it bets on the 0.15 bucket even though q = p there. Its edge ratio 1.0 exceeds the reserve R = 0.865, so it's a hedge: it pays off in a state where the main bet loses.
  - Treating each bucket as an independent binary gives Kelly (q−p)/(1−p) = 10.45% on one bucket and growth 0.01074, about 10% less.
- Simulation over 2,000 rounds × 400 paths matches theory (0.01210 vs 0.01189).
- **Noisy beliefs** (multiplicative error with sd σ on each q):

| σ | full Kelly E[growth] | P(growth<0) | half Kelly | P(<0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | +0.0089 | 1% | +0.0081 | 0% |
| 0.25 | **−0.0079** | 59% | +0.0038 | 23% |
| 0.50 | −0.0694 | 94% | −0.0121 | 67% |

  With realistic belief errors of 25%, full Kelly has *negative* expected growth. Same lesson as P78: fractional Kelly.

## Lessons
1. In multi-outcome markets, solve the joint Kelly problem. Per-bucket binary Kelly is suboptimal and ignores hedging.
2. Outcomes with q/p just above the reserve R are worth backing as hedges even at zero standalone edge.
3. The overround raises the bar: with Σp > 1, fewer outcomes clear q/p > R.
4. Belief noise kills full Kelly fast. Use ≤ ½ Kelly unless your probabilities are very precise.
