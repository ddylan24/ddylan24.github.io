# R1: Kalshi daily BTC threshold markets vs a DVOL lognormal model

**Question.** Are Kalshi's "BTC above K at 5pm ET" contracts (series KXBTCD) calibrated? Does a plain lognormal with Deribit's DVOL forecast better, and can it be traded after fees?

## Data (`code/r1_fetch.py`, `code/r1_batch.py`, `code/r1_btc_calib.py`)
- 68 settled daily events (2026-08-08 → 10-14). Kalshi's settled listing reaches back only ~2 months.
- **Strike selection:** strikes within ±6% of spot **at T−24h**, not of the settle value. Selecting on the settle value would be outcome look-ahead. That gives 2,385 markets.
- Quotes: hourly bid/ask candles via the **batch endpoint** `GET /markets/candlesticks?market_tickers=a,b,…` (one call per ~40 markets). Single-market calls get rate-limited to ~1 per 3–4 s; the batch endpoint did all of them in under 2 minutes.
- Model: P = N(d2) with spot = Deribit BTC-PERPETUAL, σ = DVOL (30-day ATM implied vol) and zero drift, at T−24h and T−6h. Kalshi settles on a 60-second average of CF BRTI.
- Inference: every strike of one day shares one BTC path, so all t-stats are **clustered by day**. That's effectively 68 independent observations.

## A look-ahead trap, caught
- Deribit `get_tradingview_chart_data` bars are **stamped at their start**, so the close is only known an hour later.
- With start stamps, the model "beat" the market at T−6h (Brier 0.025 vs 0.033), and a fee-aware rule made **+9.1¢/contract (t 3.6)**.
- Re-stamped by close time, the edge is **+0.03¢ (t 0.0)**. One hour of future BTC is a big deal over a 6-hour horizon.
- Rule: know whether your bar timestamps mark the open or the close.

## Results (correct timing)
| | T−24h | T−6h |
|---|---|---|
| quotes / days | 1,729 / 68 | 2,382 / 68 |
| Brier market vs model | **0.0994** vs 0.1051 (model worse, t 3.7) | **0.0329** vs 0.0340 (t 1.6) |
| log loss market vs model | 0.333 vs 0.364 (t 2.4) | 0.121 vs 0.122 (t 0.6) |
| calibration slope market | 0.90 ± 0.13 | **1.35 ± 0.18** |
| encompassing logit(y) ~ mkt + model | 1.82 (t 3.8) / −0.89 (t −2.0) | 0.99 (t 3.7) / 0.34 (t 1.4) |
| rule: trade when model beats quote by >3¢+fee | **−7.2¢/contract (t −2.5)** | +0.0¢ (t 0.0) |

- **The market beats a vanilla options model.** At 24h, the model adds nothing and gets negative weight. Kalshi prices already embed the skew, fat tails and intraday information (consistent with Q3, where live prices matched the smile digital to ~1.5¢). Trading against the market using flat-vol N(d2) loses about 7¢ per contract.
- **Calibration.**
  - At 24h, close to calibrated (slope 0.90). Contracts priced under 5¢ won 7.5% vs a 1.9¢ average price, but that's driven by a few big-move days (clustered).
  - At 6h the slope is 1.35, so prices are *under-confident*: 5–15¢ contracts won 4.2% vs 9.2¢, and 85–95¢ won 96.6% vs 91.2¢.
  - That's suggestive of a short-horizon favorite-longshot pattern like Kalshi weather's (N2), but 68 clustered days is too few to trade on. A recalibration rule needs an out-of-sample test on more history. That's a good job for the user's home machine (see CONTEXT §5b).

## Lessons
1. Look-ahead can hide in a timestamp convention. Check whether bars are labeled by open or close.
2. Select contracts using information available at the forecast time.
3. Kalshi crypto prices are as good as or better than a DVOL lognormal. Any edge must come from a better *distribution* (skew/term structure, as in Q3) or better calibration at short horizons, not from N(d2).
