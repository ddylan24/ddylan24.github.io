# V3: Economic-data prediction markets (CPI, jobs, unemployment, GDP)

**Data (`code/v3_econ.py`).** Polymarket public-search over closed events (CPI, inflation, jobs report, unemployment rate, payrolls, GDP, PCE) gave 193 events and 1,142 bucket markets, US and international, 2025–26. Hourly or daily history; price 1 and 7 days before the **scheduled** end (P2 look-ahead rule). Event-clustered inference.

## Calibration
| horizon | n | events | Brier skill | logit slope | intercept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T−1d | 880 | 190 | 30.1% | **1.07 ± 0.09** | −0.08 |
| T−7d | 894 | 178 | 26.4% | 1.01 ± 0.10 | −0.14 |

- **Well calibrated.** The slope is indistinguishable from 1 at both horizons; bins track hit rates within a few points.
- Skill (26–30%) is lower than Polymarket overall (41–49%). Data releases are genuinely hard to call a day ahead. Most of the information arrives at 8:30am on release day (as Q2/S7 showed for the Fed).

## A trap: "YES is overpriced" was just the vig
- Raw mean (outcome − price) at T−1d is **−1.7¢ (t −3.9)** overall and −4.2¢ (t −4.0) for unemployment. That looks like a strong, tradable bias.
- But these are multi-bucket events (median 6 buckets), and complete events' prices sum to a **mean of 1.077** (median 1.03). An overround of about 7.7¢ spread over about 6 buckets makes every YES look about 1.3¢ overpriced on average, and more in events with a bigger vig (unemployment).
- After normalizing each complete event to sum to 1, the mean bias is **0.00¢**. That's mechanical: if prices sum to 1 and exactly one bucket wins, Σ(y − p) = 0 per event. So averaging (y − p) over buckets *only* measures the overround. Calibration has to be judged by the slope, by the de-vigged distribution (R5 methods), or by sorting buckets on price.
- Tradable form: shorting every YES in an over-round event (buying all NOs) is exactly the neg-risk Dutch book of P34. It's worth Σp − 1 minus fees, bucket by bucket, and only where the books are deep enough.

## Lessons
1. Economic-data markets are well calibrated (slope ≈ 1). Their edge, if any, is fast reaction on release mornings, not a bias.
2. **Never average (outcome − price) across mutually exclusive buckets without removing the overround.** That "bias" is the vig.
