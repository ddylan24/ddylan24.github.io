# J1 — Momentum crashes (Ken French UMD, monthly 1927-01 → 2026-08; daily for vol)
Raw: 7.4%/yr, Sharpe .45, skew −3.02, kurtosis 30.4. Max drawdown 78% (trough 1939-09).
Worst months: 1932-08 −52.6% (mkt +37.1%), 1932-07 −45.6% (+33.6%), 2009-04 −34.4% (+10.2%), 1939-09 −31.4% (+16.9%), 2001-01 −25.4%, 1938-06 −24.9%. 2009 Mar–May −49.4%.
Mechanism (Daniel & Moskowitz 2016): after a bear market the loser leg is full of beaten-down high-beta stocks → MOM beta −0.64 in bear states vs +0.05 normally. When the market rebounds, the short leg rips. Bear (past 24m mkt<0) & market up: mean −2.64%/month (n=156); bear & market down +3.19%; normal +0.83%.
Constant-vol momentum (Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015): scale = 12%/σ_126d of daily UMD, lagged. 1927–2026: Sharpe .87 vs .45; skew −.40 vs −3.01; worst month −29.1% vs −52.6%; max lev 4.7x. 1927–99 1.00 vs .58; 2000–26 .40 vs .13. (BSC report .97 vs .53 for 1927–2011 ✓ consistent.)
Lesson: momentum's risk is predictable (vol is persistent; crash states identifiable); scaling by forecast vol nearly doubles Sharpe — a much stronger result than for the market itself (Module 10).
