# G1 — Time-series momentum replication
Verified benchmark: AQR "Time Series Momentum: Factors, Monthly" (aqr.com dataset, MOP 2012 updated), 1985-01 → 2026-05.
AQR TSMOM Sharpe by era: 1985–99 1.62 (18.4%/yr); 2000–09 1.13 (14.5%); 2010–26 0.40 (5.3%). Strong decay.
My version: 15 ETFs (SPY EFA EEM IWM TLT IEF LQD GLD SLV DBC USO UUP FXE FXY VNQ), Yahoo adj closes, excess over Ken French RF; monthly; signal = sign of trailing L-month excess return; position 40%/σ_ex-ante (EWMA com 60d) or equal notional; 1-month lag; 2008-01 → 2026-08.
Sharpe (gross / with 20bp per unit turnover): 1m .14/−.32 (vol-scaled); 3m .40/.16; 6m .47/.29; 9m .49/.33; 12m .43/.29. Equal-notional versions lower (12m .22/.15) → vol-scaling ~doubles Sharpe.
Correlation of my 12m vol-scaled with AQR TSMOM 2008–26: 0.79; Sharpe 0.42 vs AQR 0.41 same window. Replication validated against independent source.
Deflated Sharpe (10 variants tried): monthly SR .124 vs luck benchmark SR0 .057; PSR .97, DSR .84 — suggestive, below 95%.
Crisis alpha: 2008 +26.1% (AQR +24.6%, SPY −38.0%); 2022 +28.9% (AQR +24.7%, SPY −19.2%); Feb–Apr 2020 +8.2% (AQR +9.0%, SPY −9.6%).
Lessons: trend's value today is diversification/crisis convexity more than standalone Sharpe; short lookbacks die to costs; DSR shows even a famous strategy is only ~84% likely to beat luck on 18 years when you count 10 variants.
