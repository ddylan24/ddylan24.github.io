# X2: The cost side of Kalshi's LIP. Adverse selection at the touch

**Question.** LIP pays for resting depth. What do resting makers lose when they get filled?

## Method (`code/x2_lip_markout.py`)
- The 60 richest ≥1-day LIP markets (from W1). Public fills in the last 36 hours from `GET /trade-api/v2/markets/trades?ticker=…&min_ts=…` (fields: taker_side, yes_price_dollars, count_fp, created_time). That's 3,953 trades and 442k contracts in 51 markets that traded.
- Every fill has a resting maker on the other side.
- **Maker markout** per contract h minutes later, against the 1-minute candle mid (batch candles, at most ~4 markets per call for 36h of 1-minute bars; bigger requests come back empty):
  - taker bought YES at P → maker short YES → P − mid_{t+h}
  - taker bought NO (sold YES at P) → maker long YES → mid_{t+h} − P
  - This *includes* the half-spread the maker earns at the fill. A negative value means adverse selection exceeds the spread.

## Results
| horizon | maker markout per filled contract | total (36h) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | **−2.52¢** | −$11.1k |
| 30 min | **−2.55¢** | −$11.3k |
| 60 min | **−3.36¢** | −$14.9k |

- **Takers in these markets are informed on average.** Makers lose 2.5¢ per contract within 5 minutes (despite earning the 1¢ spread), and more by an hour.
- **Per day across the 51 markets:** maker markouts ≈ **−$7.5k/day** vs LIP pools ≈ **$11.4k/day**. Rewards cover about 1.5× the fill losses, leaving ~$3.9k/day net for all LPs combined, about a 34% margin.
  - That's consistent with W3's contest: return over costs 1/(n−1) ≈ 0.5 implies n ≈ 3 effective competitors.
- **Concentration:** the median market loses only $4/day to fills against a $112/day pool. But in 5 of 51 markets, fill losses exceed the whole pool (event-driven repricings). Most of the cost comes from a few markets with news.
- **Caveats:**
  - The trades include makers who aren't LIP participants.
  - Markouts are against the mid, not resolution.
  - 36 hours is a single window.
  - The rewards column uses the snapshot $/day of these ≥1-day programs.

## What it means for an LP
- The program is roughly **fair, not free**. Rewards ≈ 1.5× adverse selection for the incumbent pool. A newcomer taking ~20% of the score (W1) would take about 20% of both rewards and fill losses, netting perhaps $20–25/day per market in quiet markets. But one news event can wipe out weeks of rewards.
- Edges that change the math:
  1. **Avoid or pull quotes in markets with scheduled news** (the 5/51 big losers).
  2. **Quote one tick back when informed flow is high.** Credit falls to 0.5× (DF), but fills fall more.
  3. **Skew by inventory** (T2).
  4. **Prefer markets where markouts are near zero.** Measure per series before committing capital (PLAYBOOK §2.5 now has a concrete cost model).
