3) Manski bound: with risk-neutral equal-wealth traders the price π is the (1−π) quantile of beliefs,
   so the mean belief can be anywhere in (π², 2π − π²):
   price 0.05 → mean belief in (0.0025, 0.0975)
   price 0.10 → mean belief in (0.0100, 0.1900)
   price 0.30 → mean belief in (0.0900, 0.5100)
   price 0.50 → mean belief in (0.2500, 0.7500)
   price 0.90 → mean belief in (0.8100, 0.9900)
   example: 10% believe 0.90, 90% believe 0.02 → mean 0.108; Manski price 0.100; log-utility price 0.108; γ=0.2 price 0.100

4) Wealth matters: log-utility price = wealth-weighted mean. If wealth correlates with belief, price ≠ simple mean:
   corr(wealth, belief) 0.43: simple mean 0.2842, wealth-weighted 0.4785, price 0.4785
