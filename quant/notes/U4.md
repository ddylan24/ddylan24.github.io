# U4: Proper-scoring tournament with Diebold–Mariano tests, and a candidate edge

**Method.** For each model and test event, loss = log loss (and Brier). d_t = L(model) − L(benchmark), averaged per event (weather) or per day (BTC) and ordered in time. **DM = mean(d) / Newey–West SE (5 lags).** All fits use the first half of days chronologically; scores are on the second half. Script: `code/u4_tournament.py`.

### A bug caught along the way
Day labels like "26AUG08" sort as strings AUG < OCT < SEP, so my first "first half" wasn't chronological. That version showed a too-good t of −4.5 and +4.5¢ trades. Fixed by parsing dates before sorting. **Pitfall: never sort date strings.**

### Trading T−6h out of sample (cross the spread, pay the Kalshi fee)
- **Recalibration with intercept** buys YES almost exclusively: +2.6 to +4.1¢/contract, daily t 0.7–1.6.
  - Its +0.26 intercept learned BTC's first-half rally (+17%). YES rates were 0.54 vs 0.51. That's a *directional* bet, not calibration.
- **Slope-only recalibration** p' = logit⁻¹(1.21·logit p) is direction-neutral, with balanced buys and sells:
  - edge > fee: 312 buys / 289 sells, **+3.1¢/contract, daily t +3.0**
  - edge > 1¢ + fee: 134 / 129, **+4.9¢, t +2.3**

## Assessment
- **The candidate:** Kalshi's daily BTC contracts are *under-confident* 6 hours before settlement. A logit slope of about 1.2 pushes probabilities away from 50% and improves forecasts out of sample. This is consistent with R1 (slope 1.35 at 6h) and S2 (all hourly slopes > 1).
- **Why not trade it yet:**
  1. Only 34 test days.
  2. The slope-only form was chosen *after* seeing the intercept problem. It's well motivated, but it's a researcher degree of freedom (≈6 variants tried in this arena; Bonferroni hurdle ≈2.6, which t 3.0 barely clears).
  3. Fills assume the hourly candle's closing bid/ask with no depth limit.
  4. A single regime (Aug–Oct 2026).
