# R6: Excess belief movement (Augenblick & Rabin 2021, QJE)

**The identity.** If a price π_t is a martingale (a rational, calibrated forecast) that ends at the outcome y ∈ {0,1}, then
`E[m] = E[r]`, with movement `m = Σ (π_{t+1} − π_t)²` and uncertainty reduction `r = π_0(1−π_0) − y(1−y) = π_0(1−π_0)`.
- Proof, one step: E[(Δπ)²] = E[π_{t+1}²] − E[π_t²] by the martingale property, and u = π − π² gives E[u_t − u_{t+1}] = E[π_{t+1}²] − E[π_t²]. Summing over steps telescopes.
- It holds at **any sampling frequency** (a subsampled martingale is still a martingale), needs no model of the information, and gives one number: **Σm/Σr = 1** under rationality.
- More movement than that means beliefs swing more than the information justifies (overreaction or noise). Less means underreaction.

## Test (`code/r6_movement.py [skip_days]`)
1,625 resolved Polymarket markets (top ~2,100 by volume, daily CLOB prices, data from P2). Each path runs from the first price to **one day before close** (avoiding the early-close look-ahead found in P2), then jumps to the outcome. Standard errors use an event-clustered bootstrap.

| sample | Σm/Σr | ± se |
|---|---|---|
| all | **1.31** | 0.06 |
| skip first 3 days (arbitrary opening prices) | 1.38 | 0.07 |
| skip first 7 days | 1.38 | 0.06 |
| horizon <2 weeks / 2w–2m / 2–6m / >6m | 1.30 / 1.68 / 1.06 / 1.23 | 0.10 / 0.12 / 0.07 / 0.11 |
| volume terciles low / mid / high | 1.31 / 1.34 / 1.28 | ~0.09 |
| start price <10% / 10–30% / 30–70% / 70–90% | 1.48 / 1.30 / 1.26 / 1.55 | |

- **Prices move about 30–40% more than rational updating allows, at 5+ standard errors.** That's robust to dropping opening days and shows no volume gradient.
- A simulated Bayesian learner with the same path lengths and 0.5¢ quote noise gives ≈1.02, so microstructure noise doesn't explain it.
- Daily changes have mean lag-1 autocorrelation −0.08, a little reversal, consistent with partial overreaction.
- The final jump (price one day before close → outcome) is 0.40–0.47 of r. Longshot starts have the most excess (1.48). This is the same fact as the **reverse favorite-longshot pattern in P2**: cheap YES contracts win more often than priced, so the paths end with big upward jumps. Miscalibration at the start shows up as excess movement.
- Caveats:
  - The sample is the top markets by volume, and volume may select for dramatic markets, though there's no gradient within the sample.
  - Daily snapshots of a "p" that may be a last trade or mid.
  - Prices embed time value (P34), a small effect.

## Lessons
1. A one-line, model-free rationality test for any probability series: Σ(Δπ)² vs π_0(1−π_0). Use it on your own forecasts too. A forecaster whose m/r ≫ 1 is jumpy, and one with ≪ 1 is sluggish.
2. Polymarket prices are good forecasts (Brier skill 41–49%) but too jumpy. The excess sits mostly in mid-length (2-week to 2-month) markets and in longshots.
3. A tradable version would fade large daily moves. The −0.08 autocorrelation is small next to a 1¢ spread on daily changes, so test it with costs before believing it.
