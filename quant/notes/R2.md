# R2 (+S4): Skill vs luck among Polymarket traders

**Question.** Polymarket leaderboards show wallets with millions in profit. Is that skill? And do takers as a group win or lose against makers?

## Data (`code/r2_fetch.py`, `code/r2_skill.py`)
- The 400 highest-volume binary markets resolved in 2026, with up to 4,000 most recent taker trades each from data-api (`side` = taker side).
- After dropping trades in the final hour before close (often after the outcome is known): 319 markets, **1.07M trades, 235,867 wallets, $781M notional**.
- Rate limits: the data-api returns {"error": "Too Many Requests"} (a dict, not a list). Handle it, and use curl with hard timeouts, since urllib hung on a slow socket.
- Caveats: taker fills only (maker legs aren't attributed to wallets) and the most recent ≤4,000 trades per market.

## Method
- P&L per wallet-market = net YES shares × outcome − net cost.
- **First attempt (wrong):** z = P&L / √(n²p(1−p)) per wallet. Wallets that round-trip have n ≈ 0 and deterministic trading P&L, so z exploded (sd 9,070). Don't do this.
- **Correct null:** each market's outcome is drawn **once for all wallets** from Bernoulli(the market's volume-weighted taker price), 500 times. Real P&L is ranked against the simulated P&L to get a p-value per wallet. Sharing the draw keeps the true cross-wallet correlation: everyone in a market faces the same outcome. A placebo with fake outcomes checks the whole pipeline.

## Results
- **Skill tests (38,193 wallets with ≥3 markets):**
  - Only 0.10% have p < 0.05; the placebo gives 2.15%.
  - **BH-FDR at 5%: 0 skilled wallets** (placebo also 0).
  - The **top-10 earners ($0.17M–$5.1M) all have p between 0.06 and 0.84**. The $5.1M wallet earned it on 7 markets (p 0.29).
  - With a few hundred markets whose outcomes are shared, even huge P&Ls are statistically indistinguishable from big bets that happened to win. The test is conservative (placebo 2% < 5%) because market-level outcome risk dominates.
- **Takers as a group:** total P&L +$8.4M vs a null mean of +$11.4M ± $16.2M, so no detectable transfer between takers and makers before fees and rewards. (The S4 question isn't answerable with this data; it needs maker-side attribution.)
- **Typical wallet:** median ROI on traded notional is −0.16%, and 45.9% of wallets are profitable.
- **Persistence (2,111 wallets active in both halves, split at 2026-05-14):**
  - Spearman(ROI early, ROI late) = **+0.071 (p 0.001)**.
  - Late median ROI by early-ROI quintile, worst to best: −0.54%, −0.50%, −0.03%, +0.84%, **+1.29%**. Share profitable late: 46%, 43%, 48%, 58%, 54%.
  - So there is weak but real persistence: past winners do slightly better.
  - Late *total* dollars are dominated by a few big bets (the worst early quintile made +$648k late), so dollar P&L is a noisy skill measure. ROI ranks are better.

## Lessons
1. **A leaderboard is not evidence of skill.** With shared outcomes and concentrated bets, no single wallet clears a multiple-testing bar.
2. The right null for skill shares the same outcome draw across all traders. Per-trader independent nulls overstate significance enormously.
3. Skill shows up as **small, persistent ROI** (about 1–2% of notional for the top quintile), not as giant P&L. If copying wallets, rank by risk-adjusted ROI across many markets, never by total profit.
