# W3: The economics of a proportional liquidity subsidy (Kalshi LIP as a Tullock contest)

## Model
- n liquidity providers. LP i rests depth q_i (at the reference price, so full credit) and gets reward share q_i/Q of pool R per day, with Q = Σq.
- Per contract-day cost k covers capital, adverse selection on fills, inventory/jump risk and operations.
- Profit: π_i = R·q_i/Q − k·q_i. This is a **Tullock contest**.
- Best response: q_i = √(R·O/k) − O, where O = others' depth.
- Symmetric Nash:
  - **Q* = R(n−1)/(n·k)**, q* = Q*/n
  - **π* = R/n²**
  - **return on cost = 1/(n−1)**
- **Free entry (n → ∞): R = k·Q.** The subsidy is entirely dissipated into extra depth. That's the exchange's goal: it buys liquidity, and competitive LPs earn only their costs.

Numerical check (`code/w3_lip_equilibrium.py`, R = $100, k = 2¢): best-response iteration matches the formulas exactly. n = 2/3/5/10 gives depth 2,500/3,333/4,000/4,500, profit per LP $25/11.1/4/1, and return on cost 100%/50%/25%/11%.

## What live books imply (169 two-sided programs from the LIP census)
- From the newcomer simulation, existing effective depth per side ≈ q(1−s)/s: median **1,327 contracts**, barely above the median Target of 1,000.
- If free entry held, k = R/(2·depth): median **11.2¢ per contract-day** (IQR 2.6–36¢).
- Capital cost alone (10%/yr on about $0.50 per contract) is 0.014¢/day. **Implied k is about 800× the capital cost.**
- So depth isn't limited by capital. Some mix of:
  1. **Adverse selection and inventory risk are large**, especially in hourly weather and short-dated markets (Q2/S10/T2).
  2. **Few active LPs** (small n gives return 1/(n−1)). Many programs look under-contested, consistent with the 2–15%/day newcomer estimates.
  3. **Short windows:** hourly programs make "per day" rates overstate the pool, which inflates the implied k for those markets.
- Telling these apart needs time-series data on depth and fills, which W1 starts and `live/capture.py` continues.

## Lessons
1. A proportional subsidy is a contest. Its rents shrink like 1/n² per LP, and with free entry it's fully competed away.
2. To profit, you need either a cost advantage (lower adverse selection through better pricing or news handling, cheaper capital) or entry into under-contested pools early. Measure n (distinct depth providers over time) before committing capital.
