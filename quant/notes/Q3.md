# Q3: Binary (digital) options math vs crypto threshold prediction markets

**Question.** "Bitcoin above $K on date T" contracts on Polymarket and Kalshi are cash-or-nothing digital options. Do their prices agree with the probability implied by the Deribit BTC option surface?

## The math
- **Cash-or-nothing call** (pays $1 if S_T > K), Black-76 on forward F with total variance w = σ²T:
  `D = e^{−rT} N(d2)`, `d2 = [ln(F/K) − w/2]/√w`. N(d2) is the **risk-neutral** P(S_T > K), not the real-world one.
- **Breeden–Litzenberger / replication.** `D(K) = −∂C/∂K`. A tight call spread `[C(K−h) − C(K+h)] / 2h` *is* the digital, so it can be hedged with vanillas.
- **The smile matters.** With σ = σ(K): `D = e^{−rT}[N(d2) − F·φ(d1)·√T · ∂σ/∂K]` (the vega term). BTC has put skew (∂σ/∂K < 0 below spot), so the true digital sits **above** flat-vol N(d2) for strikes below spot. Here the gap was up to **5.7¢**.
- **Digital delta = density.** `∂D/∂S ≈ φ(d2)/(S σ√T)`. At the money with a 2-day horizon and 40% vol, that's about **4¢ per $100** move in BTC. Small differences in spot source, quote currency (USDT) or carry move these contracts by cents. Gamma blows up into expiry, which is why digitals are hard to hedge.
- **Time interpolation.** Deribit expires at 08:00 UTC, Polymarket at 16:00 UTC (Binance BTCUSDT 1-minute close at noon ET) and Kalshi at 21:00 UTC (60-second average of CF BRTI before 5pm ET). I interpolated total variance linearly in time at fixed log-moneyness, with w = 0 at T = 0, and the forward linearly.

## Data and method
- **Deribit** `public/get_book_summary_by_currency?currency=BTC&kind=option`: mark_iv and underlying_price per expiry; 449 OTM options across 11 expiries. Smile per expiry as total variance vs log-moneyness, either piecewise linear or a smooth quartic fit for |k| < 0.3 (results agree).
- **Polymarket** events `bitcoin-above-on-<month>-<day>-<year>` (11 strikes per day). **Kalshi** series `KXBTCD` (field `floor_strike`).
- **Adjustments.** Polymarket strikes are in USDT, so K_USD = K × USDT/USD = K × 0.99873 (Kraken). The forward was scaled by the Deribit index move between snapshots.
- Script: `code/q3_digitals.py` (`--smooth` for the quartic smile). Snapshot 2026-10-14 22:16 UTC, BTC ≈ $81.6k, ATM vol 32–38%.

## Results
| | Polymarket | Kalshi |
|---|---|---|
| strikes with 3% < p < 97% | 34 | 45 |
| mean (mid − options) | −0.6¢ | −0.2¢ |
| mean \|diff\| | 1.4¢ | 1.3¢ |
| RMSE vs smile digital | **1.74¢** | **1.55¢** |
| RMSE vs flat-vol N(d2) | 3.36¢ | 3.23¢ |
| options prob inside bid–ask | 56% | 24% |
| median spread | 1.0¢ | 1.0¢ |

- **The venues price the skew.** Their mids are twice as close to the smile-consistent digital as to flat N(d2). The crowd (or the market makers behind it) is effectively running an options model.
- **Residual pattern.**
  - Near the money (30–70%), the venues sit 2–3¢ *below* options, while wings agree within ~0.5¢.
  - A single forward shift per expiry removes most of it, cutting RMSE from 1.4–2.2¢ to **0.4–1.2¢**, about one spread.
  - The implied shift is −$80 to −$170 USD and grows with horizon, roughly tracking Deribit's carry (forward − index: +$18 to +$82).
  - So **prediction-market traders center on spot, not the futures forward.** The futures premium (crypto funding/basis, about 6% annualized here) is a risk premium (see K12) that these markets don't price in.
- **Is it tradable?** The best "executable vs options" gap was 2–3¢ on a few near-ATM strikes. Hedging requires Deribit call spreads with a different settlement time (08:00 vs 16:00/21:00), a different index (Deribit USD vs Binance USDT vs CF BRTI), fees on both venues, and capital split across venues. On top of that, the "mispricing" is just the carry premium, which you'd be *buying* and which isn't risk-free. This is relative value with basis risk, not arbitrage.

## Lessons
1. Price binary contracts with the **smile-consistent digital −∂C/∂K**, never flat-vol N(d2). The skew error is several cents.
2. Before calling a binary mispriced, reconcile the **settlement index, quote currency, timestamp and forward vs spot**. Each of these is worth cents because digital delta is so large.
3. Crypto threshold markets are efficiently linked to the options market to within about one spread. The residual is the funding/carry premium.
4. Market making in these contracts amounts to quoting Deribit-implied digitals with a spot-based center, plus inventory hedged with perps or call spreads.
