# S9: How volatile should a probability be? Diffusion of a prediction-market price

## Theory
Suppose the outcome is 1{X_T > 0}, where the "state" X is a Brownian motion with unit variance per unit time and τ = T − t is the time left.
- The fair price is **p_t = N(X_t/√τ)**.
- Itô: dp = [N'·dX/√τ] + [∂_t terms + ½N''·dt/τ]. The drift terms cancel exactly (p is a martingale), leaving
  **dp = φ(N⁻¹(p)) / √τ · dW**.
- So a daily change has variance **Var(Δp) ≈ φ(N⁻¹(p))² · Δt/τ**.
  - Largest at p = ½ (φ(0)² = 0.159) and tiny near 0 or 1.
  - It blows up as τ → 0, because a coin-flip contract must resolve in a burst at the end.
- This is the "digital option gamma" of Q3 in probability units, and the quadratic-variation budget of R6: E[Σ(Δp)²] = p₀(1 − p₀).

## Test (`code/s9_prob_vol.py`)
- 103,935 daily changes from 543 resolved Polymarket events (P2 data): price 2–98%, at least 1 day to the scheduled end, last day before close excluded.
- Cell means of (Δp)² by price × time-to-end bucket. Weighted regression: log E[Δp²] = a + β₁ log φ(N⁻¹p)² + β₂ log(1/τ).

| | estimate | theory |
|---|---|---|
| β₁ (price shape) | **0.99 ± 0.05** | 1 |
| β₂ (time to end) | **0.53 ± 0.05** | 1 |
| R² | 0.93 | |

- **The price shape is exactly as theory predicts.** By price bucket, the actual sd of daily changes is 1.7 / 3.4 / 4.7 / 5.9 / 4.9 / 3.6¢, against the scaled theory of 1.6 / 3.4 / 5.0 / 5.9 / 5.1 / 3.5¢.
- **Time dependence is only half as steep** (τ^−0.53, not τ^−1). Real information doesn't arrive as a steady Brownian signal ending at T. Many markets get resolved by discrete news, and much of the variance happens weeks out rather than piling up at expiry. "Information time" runs about like √(calendar time).
- **The exception is near-certain contracts.** In the 90–98¢ bucket, the actual sd is 4.1¢ against 1.8¢ predicted, more than double. Near-certainties suffer jumps (surprise reversals). That's the same fat tail that made "buy 95¢ favorites" lose 3% vs T-bills in S3.

## Uses
1. **Market making:** set the spread and inventory limits proportional to φ(N⁻¹(p))/τ^{~0.5}. Quote wider near 50% and close to resolution, but *not* tight on 95¢ contracts: their jump risk is twice the diffusion estimate.
2. **Position risk:** the 1-day sd of a $10k YES position at p = 0.5 with τ = 30 days is about $10k × 5¢ / 0.5 ≈ $1k. Use the table, not intuition.
3. **Test:** anomalous volatility relative to φ(N⁻¹(p)) flags news or manipulation.
