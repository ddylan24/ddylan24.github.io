# X1: Short LIP programs (hourly / 15-minute): rewards vs fill costs by series

> **CORRECTION (Y1, 7 days marked to settlement):** the FX 15-minute maker profits below don't hold up. Over a week, maker fill P&L to settlement was +0.09¢ (EUR, t 0.2), +1.03¢ (JPY, t 1.0) and −0.36¢ (AUD, t −0.7), i.e. zero. Read the FX rows as a one-day fluke from a 5-minute markout. The robust takeaway is that fills roughly break even in short programs, so rewards are close to pure profit.

**Method (`code/x1_short_lip.py`).**
- Short programs (≤24h) that ended in the last 24 hours, in the 8 series with the biggest pools. Up to 60 markets per series, 1,332 programs in all.
- Every public fill during each program window. Maker markout vs the 1-minute mid 5 minutes later, or the settlement value if the market settled first. This includes the spread the maker earns.
- Compared with the program pools for the same markets.

## Interpretation
- **Short programs are much more attractive than long ones** (X2: long-program pools were only 1.5× fill losses).
  - **15-minute FX:** takers *lose* to makers by 1–3¢ per contract. That looks like retail directional betting on short FX moves. Makers collect the reward *and* positive fill P&L.
  - **Hourly temperature, Rotten Tomatoes, approval brackets:** small adverse selection (≤0.8¢) against pools 6–10× larger.
  - **15-minute crypto is the exception:** −3.8¢ per contract. Arbitrageurs watch spot BTC and pick off stale quotes; U2 showed Kalshi crypto quotes reprice within the minute. Pool ≈ losses.
- This lines up with S6/U2: adverse selection is high where public, fast-moving information exists (crypto spot), and low where takers have no information edge (FX 15-min retail, entertainment).
- **Caveats:**
  - One 24-hour window, capped at 60 markets per series.
  - Markouts at 5 minutes vs mid. For 15-minute markets the final settlement dominates the risk, so inventory carried to settlement isn't fully captured.
  - Fills include non-LIP makers.
  - The pool per series is shared among all qualifying LPs (W1/W3 shares ~20–35% for a newcomer).
  - Edges like this attract competition (W3).
