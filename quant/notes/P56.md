# P5 — Kelly for binary contracts
Buy YES at price p, belief q. Win returns (1−p)/p per $ staked. G(f) = q·ln(1+f(1−p)/p) + (1−q)·ln(1−f) ⇒ f* = (q − p)/(1 − p). (NO side: f* = (p − q)/p.)
Examples: q .55 p .50 → 10.0% stake, growth .0050/bet; q .60 → 20%, .0201; q .10 p .05 → 5.3%, .0207; q .30 p .20 → 12.5%; q .97 p .95 → 40% (!), .0049. Half Kelly keeps 71–80% of growth.
Fees (share of payout φ ⇒ effective price p + φ(1−p)): q .55 p .50: 0% → f 10.0%, g .0050; 1% → 9.1%, .0041; 2% → 8.2%, .0032 — a 2% fee eats 36% of growth.
Time value: same 5¢ edge, growth .0050 per bet: resolving in 1 week → 26%/yr; 13 wk → 2.0%/yr; 1 yr → 0.5%/yr; 2 yr → 0.25%/yr vs T-bills 4.25%. Long-dated edges are worthless unless huge.
Estimation error sim: true q .53 vs p .50, trader's belief noisy ±5¢, 2,000 bets, 3,000 paths: full Kelly on estimates → median log-wealth −3.01, P(lose) 72%, P(lose 90%+) 55%; half → +1.55, 26%, 5%; quarter → +1.57, 10%, 0%. Noisy beliefs make "full Kelly" effectively over-Kelly.
# P6 — Literature
- Wolfers & Zitzewitz (2004, JEP) "Prediction Markets": overview; prices ≈ mean beliefs.
- Berg, Nelson & Rietz (2008, IJF): Iowa Electronic Markets vs 964 polls 1988–2004: market closer to outcome 74% of the time; significantly better than polls >100 days out.
- Page & Clemen (2013, Economic Journal 123(568)): well calibrated near expiry; favorite–longshot-type bias (prices pulled toward 50%) grows with time to expiration; exploiting it pays only at low discount rates (time value).
- Snowberg & Wolfers (2010, JPE 118(4)): favorite–longshot bias in betting better explained by probability misperception (prospect theory) than risk-love.
- Saguillo et al. (2025, arXiv:2508.03474): ~$40M realized Polymarket arbitrage; LLM dependency detection.
Connection to our data: our YES underpricing (~4–5¢) and long-horizon pull toward 50% are consistent with Page & Clemen + time value; tradability hinges on horizon and fees.
