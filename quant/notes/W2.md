# W2: Kalshi Liquidity Incentive Program vs Polymarket Liquidity Rewards

**Sources:** Kalshi help center and CFTC filings (notes/LIP.md); Polymarket docs, "Liquidity Rewards" (docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards, read 2026-10-15).

## Polymarket (from the docs)
- Per market: a max qualifying spread v (cents from the size-cutoff-adjusted midpoint), a min qualifying size, a daily reward allocation, and an in-game multiplier b.
- **Order score S(v, s) = ((v − s)/v)² · b**, quadratic in distance s from the *adjusted midpoint*.
- Q_one = Σ S·(YES bids) + Σ S·(NO asks); Q_two = Σ S·(YES asks) + Σ S·(NO bids).
- **Q_min** = max(min(Q_one, Q_two), max(Q_one/c, Q_two/c)) with c = 3 when the midpoint is in [0.10, 0.90]. Outside that band, min(Q_one, Q_two): **two-sided only near 0 or 1**.
- Normalized per sample; sampled **once per minute** at a random offset; the epoch is one UTC day; paid daily at midnight UTC; minimum payout $1.
- Past example: $1M of extra August rewards for crypto 5m/15m/4h TWAP markets (ended).

## Side-by-side
| | Kalshi LIP | Polymarket Liquidity Rewards |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor for distance | **the book itself** (reference = level reaching Target/5) | **the midpoint** (size-cutoff adjusted) |
| Distance penalty | geometric: DF^ticks, DF 0.4–0.5 | quadratic: ((v − s)/v)², zero beyond v |
| Depth requirement | snapshot counts only if *both* sides reach Target (300–2,500) | min size per order; no market-wide target |
| Two-sidedness | market-level (both sides must be deep, from anyone); each side scored separately, so a one-sided LP still earns that side's share | per LP: min(Q_one, Q_two); one-sided earns 1/3 in the 10–90% band, nothing near 0 or 1 |
| Sampling | every second (random within the second) | every minute (random offset) |
| Pools | $1–1,000/day per market; 5,734 live programs (~$324k/day) | per-market daily allocation (see the market's reward settings) |
| Payment | after the period ends, ≥$1 | daily, ≥$1 |

## Implications for an LP (quant view)
- **Polymarket pays for tightness relative to fair value.** Quotes must sit near the midpoint to score, so every reward dollar comes with fill risk at near-fair prices. The optimal quote balances the (1 − s/v)² decay against adverse selection, which falls with distance.
- **Kalshi pays for being at the top of whatever book exists.** In a thin book, the best bid can be far from fair value and still earn full credit: you're paid for depth, not tightness. Competition (outbidding by a tick resets the reference) is what pulls quotes inward, the Tullock dynamics of W3.
  - Practical consequence: in under-contested Kalshi pools, the best risk-adjusted play is often a *wide but top-of-book* two-sided quote. On Polymarket, wide quotes earn nothing.
- **Two-sidedness differs.** Polymarket forces each LP to be balanced (min of the two sides). Kalshi only needs the *market* to be two-sided, so an LP can earn on one side while others hold the other side up. But if the other side drops below Target, the whole snapshot is excluded for everyone (W1 measures how often that happens).
- **Snapshot frequency:** Kalshi's 1-second sampling makes flickering quotes (posting only around snapshot times) impractical, since the times are random within each second. Polymarket's 1-minute random sampling also blocks deterministic timing.
- Both programs are subsidies whose rents compete away (W3). The durable edge is lower adverse selection: better fair-value models (Q3 for BTC binaries, S6/S8 for information) and pulling quotes before scheduled news (Q2).
