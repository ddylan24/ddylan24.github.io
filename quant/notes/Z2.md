# Z2: Queue position and passive fill probability on Kalshi (15-minute markets, 7 days)

**Method (`code/z2_queue.py`).**
- From the Y1 public fills, a "visit" is a maximal run of consecutive trades in a market at the same YES price with the same taker side.
- An order behind A contracts (FIFO) fills only if visit volume V > A.
- "Trade-through" = the next visit (same market, same taker side) prints at a worse price for the passive side, so the old level was emptied.

## Results
| series | visits | visit volume median / mean / p90 | P(fill) behind A = 100 / 500 / 1,000 / 5,000 | trade-through after a visit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR/USD 15m | 73.7k | 10 / 34 / 84 | 7.4% / 0.4% / 0.1% / 0% | **40.1%** |
| USD/JPY 15m | 66.7k | 10 / 31 / 75 | 6.5% / 0.3% / 0.1% / 0% | **40.1%** |
| BTC 15m | 1.18M | 49 / 495 / 762 | 38% / 14% / 7.5% / 1.2% | 29.5% |

## What it means
- **Depth isn't consumed, it's cancelled.** In FX 15m a typical visit trades about 10 contracts, while LIP books hold 1,000+ at the touch (W1, Targets of 300–2,500). Yet 40% of visits are followed by trades at the next worse level. The only way that happens is that resting makers **pull their quotes** when the price moves.
- So for an LIP quoter, **queue position barely protects you**. The fills you get are the ones where the price moves and you *didn't* cancel in time: the stale-quote pickoffs measured as adverse selection in X2 and T2.
- The economics become a **latency race**: a maker that cancels as fast as the others when the underlying moves earns the reward with very few fills (P(fill) < 0.5% behind 500 contracts). A slow maker inherits the trade-throughs.
- BTC 15m is deeper and more active (visit mean 495), so queue fills matter more there.
- **For `live/lip_paper.py`:** its conservative rule (fill only on a strict trade-through, or volume beyond the queue at our price) matches this structure. It will mostly register trade-through fills, which is exactly the realistic risk for a slow, polling quoter. A real quoter must cancel on underlying moves (FX spot or BTC ticks), which requires streaming data on the home machine.
