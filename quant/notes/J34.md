# J3 — Low-beta / low-volatility anomaly (Ken French beta- and variance-sorted deciles, VW, 1963–2026)
CAPM: E[r_i]−r_f = β_i·(E[r_m]−r_f). Observed slope of avg excess return on realized β across beta deciles: 3.4% per unit β vs market premium 7.2% → security market line too flat (Black, Jensen & Scholes 1972; Frazzini & Pedersen 2014).
Beta deciles: Lo β .59 → 6.8%/yr, α +2.5%, Sharpe .57; mid β 1.02 → 7.6%, α +.3%, .46; Hi β 1.60 → 8.7%, α −2.9%, .30.
BAB-style (low-β levered to β=1 minus high-β scaled to β=1, no financing spread): +6.0%/yr, Sharpe .32, t 2.51.
Variance deciles: lowest var 6.0%, α +1.7%, Sharpe .51; highest var 1.8%/yr, β 1.68, α −10.3%/yr, Sharpe .06 (Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang 2006 idiosyncratic-vol puzzle; lottery demand).
Explanation: leverage constraints (investors who can't borrow buy high-β instead), lottery preferences. Implementation needs leverage/shorting at reasonable cost.
# J4 — James–Stein shrinkage of Sharpe ratios (30 KF industries)
θ̂_JS = mean + B(θ̂ − mean), B = max(0, 1 − σ²_noise/Var(θ̂)). Sharpe noise var ≈ 1/years.
1927–1976 (50 yrs): variance of industry Sharpes .0080 < noise .0198 → B=0: dispersion fully explainable by luck.
Predicting 1977–2026: raw MSE .0525; grand mean .0502; JS .0502; corr(first-half, second-half Sharpe) .22.
Lesson: when estimates' spread ≈ noise, shrink to the average; ranking assets/strategies on past Sharpe mostly ranks luck.
