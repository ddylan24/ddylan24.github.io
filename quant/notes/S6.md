# S6: Murphy decomposition of the Brier score, for every forecast dataset in this project

**Identity (Murphy 1973).** Group forecasts into bins k with n_k forecasts, mean forecast p̄_k and hit rate ȳ_k; overall base rate ȳ.
`Brier = REL − RES + UNC (+ a small within-bin term)`
- REL = Σ n_k (p̄_k − ȳ_k)²/N is **calibration error**. It's the only part a recalibration map can remove.
- RES = Σ n_k (ȳ_k − ȳ)²/N is **resolution**: how well the forecasts separate events that happen from ones that don't. This is *information*.
- UNC = ȳ(1−ȳ) is how hard the base rate makes the problem.
- Brier skill = 1 − BS/UNC ≈ (RES − REL)/UNC.

## What it says
- **Calibration error is 0.5–3.5% of the Brier score everywhere.** Perfect recalibration would improve Polymarket's 30-day Brier by at most 0.0034, against 0.07 of resolution.
  - The biases studied all day (P2 reverse FLB, N2 weather FLB, S3 favorites) are statistically real but small next to the information already in prices. That's why they're hard to trade after costs.
- Polymarket's resolution rises as resolution approaches (0.071 → 0.095), while calibration error falls (0.0034 → 0.0016). Information arrives and prices get both sharper and better calibrated.
- The Kalshi BTC market beats the DVOL model on **both** parts at 24h: lower REL (0.0023 vs 0.0054) *and* higher RES (0.152 vs 0.149). At 6h, RES is identical and only REL differs.
- Kalshi weather has very low REL (0.0005) but modest RES. It's well calibrated but not very informative, since next-day temperature buckets are hard.
- Caveat: skill depends on which contracts you include (adding far-from-spot strikes inflates RES), so compare forecasters on the *same* set.

## Lessons
1. To beat a market you need **resolution** (new information), not just recalibration. Recalibration's ceiling is REL, about 0.002.
2. Report REL and RES separately whenever you evaluate a model. Brier alone hides which one you're good at.
