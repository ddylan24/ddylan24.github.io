# S1: Fading large daily moves on Polymarket

**Motivation.** R6 found prices move 31–38% more than rational updating allows, with daily-change autocorrelation of −0.08. Does trading against big moves pay after costs?

## Method (`code/s1_fade.py`)
- 1,625 resolved markets with daily prices (P2). Signal: a one-day move |Δp| ≥ 5/10/20¢ with the new price between 5¢ and 95¢. Skip the first 3 days and the final day before close.
- Take the opposite side at the new price. Cost is a 1¢ half-spread each way: 2¢ for 1- and 5-day holds, 1¢ to resolution.
- t-stats clustered by event (393 events). There are 9 variants, so the Bonferroni hurdle is |t| ≈ 2.8.

## Results (¢ per $1 contract)
| move | hold | n | gross | t | net | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥5¢ | 1d / 5d / resolution | 5.8k / 5.4k / 5.9k | +0.9 / +1.8 / +1.8 | 4.8 / 6.1 / 3.5 | −1.1 / −0.2 / +0.8 | −6.4 / −0.7 / 1.6 |
| ≥10¢ | 1d / 5d / resolution | 1.9k / 1.8k / 2.0k | +1.4 / +3.1 / +2.6 | 3.7 / 5.8 / 2.6 | −0.6 / +1.1 / +1.6 | −1.5 / 2.0 / 1.6 |
| ≥20¢ | 1d / 5d / resolution | 460 / 382 / 478 | +2.7 / +5.2 / +4.4 | 3.0 / 4.1 / 2.5 | +0.7 / **+3.2** / +3.4 | 0.7 / **2.5** / 2.0 |

- **Overreaction is real.** Gross reversal is highly significant and grows with the size of the move, about 25% of the move over 5 days. That confirms R6 from a trading angle. It's symmetric for up and down moves and the same before and after mid-2025.
- **After costs it's marginal.** Only ≥20¢ moves held 5 days to resolution clear 2¢ of costs, at t 2.0–2.5. That's below the Bonferroni bar for 9 variants.
- Execution is the real problem. Right after a 20¢ jump, books are thin and wide: in M3 a $10k order cost 18.6¢ in thin markets. Daily "prices" are snapshots that may include stale prints. Treat this as **suggestive, not tradable as is.**
- Next step: re-test on tick/book data (taker trades + CLOB books, R4 method), ideally on the user's home machine with longer history (CONTEXT §5b).

## Lessons
1. The statistical edge (gross t 6) and the economic edge (net t ~2) are different things. Always report both.
2. Overreaction edges live exactly where liquidity is worst: right after big moves.
